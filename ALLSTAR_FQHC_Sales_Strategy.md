# ALLSTAR Partners | FQHC Sales Strategy

*Prepared November 3, 2026*

## Our Direction

We win community health centers by earning their trust, not by selling tools. ALLSTAR is the managed IT and cybersecurity partner that keeps care moving, makes risk visible, and builds a practical resilience program sized to each center.

**Core message:** *Keep care moving. Make IT risk visible. Build a practical security and resilience program that fits your center's realities.*

**Entry motion:** FQHC IT & Cyber Resilience Review → prioritized 90-day roadmap → recurring managed services.

**Ground rules**
- Lead with continuity, predictable support, evidence of progress. Never fear.
- Never claim ALLSTAR makes a center "HIPAA compliant." Each organization owns its risk analysis; we support it.
- No claim (24/7 SOC, pen testing, vCISO, "HIPAA-compliant MSP") until delivery leadership confirms service, staffing, evidence, and contract terms.

## 1. Who We Serve

**Market:** 1,356 HRSA Health Center Program awardees, 32.7M patients (2025 UDS). Finite, named-account market. Tier it; don't spray it.

**Ideal customer:** HRSA-funded FQHC or look-alike. Multi-site or growing. Lean IT team. Executive sponsor worried about downtime, cyber risk, audit readiness, or uneven support.

**Signals to watch** (validate each; they are hypotheses)
- New sites, merger, expansion, heavy hiring
- Open IT/security role; one-person IT dependence
- EHR/cloud migration, telehealth growth, aging infrastructure
- Recent incident, cyber-insurance renewal, board security review
- PCA / HCCN relationships

| Tier | Profile | Volume | Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Complex multi-site, active trigger, IT team needs augmentation | 25–40 | Executive-led, tailored workshop |
| **2** | Mid-sized, lean IT, standardized environment | 50–100 | Phased package, low-friction assessment |
| **3** | PCAs, HCCNs, regional networks | Selective | Education, referral, partnership |

**Geography:** Start where we deliver reliably today. Cluster territories. Expand after references and economics are proven.

### Buying Committee

| Role | Cares about |
|---|---|
| CEO / ED | Care continuity, organizational risk, minimal disruption |
| CFO / COO | Predictable cost, clear scope, grant-budget fit |

Multi-thread every deal: executive sponsor, operational owner, technical evaluator.

## 2. Positioning

> For community health centers that need dependable technology and stronger cyber resilience without building every capability in-house, ALLSTAR provides bundled managed IT and security, collaborative discovery, and a practical roadmap. Unlike a ticket-only provider or tool reseller, we scope support around the center's operating environment.

**Five pillars**
1. **Care continuity.** EHR, identity, network, phones, backup, user support.
2. **Right-sized risk reduction.** Findings become owners, milestones, budget choices.
3. **One accountable partner.** Support, systems, monitoring, security, recovery, roadmap.
4. **FQHC-aware operations.** Multi-site clinics, lean teams, clinical workflow. Validate in discovery.
5. **Evidence and transparency.** Clear boundaries, SLAs, escalation, reporting.

HHS Cybersecurity Performance Goals are a helpful conversation framework. They are voluntary, not a compliance certification.

### Proof Assets to Build Before Scaling

- FQHC service map (capabilities → outcomes → boundaries)
- Anonymized risk-to-roadmap sample report
- Sample service review dashboard
- FQHC reference (client permission, verifiable outcomes only)
- Due-diligence packet: BAA template, security questionnaire, insurance, subcontractors, incident notification, data handling, access controls
- Service responsibility matrix (ALLSTAR / center / other vendors)

## 3. Offers

*Product concepts for scoping, not representations that every capability is in a standard package.*

**Entry: FQHC IT & Cyber Resilience Review**
- Time-boxed; paid or selectively sponsored. If free, strict scope; disqualify full-audit seekers.
- Scope: leadership interviews, IT/service-desk baseline, critical-service map, high-level review of identity, endpoints, patching, backup/recovery, incident readiness, vendors, current risk-analysis process.
- Deliverables: executive summary, prioritized action register, recovery discussion, responsibility gaps, immediate / near-term / strategic recommendations, leadership readout, 90-day roadmap.
- Not legal advice, audit opinion, pen test, or certification. Agree on secure data handling first.

**Recurring**
- **Managed IT & Security Foundation.** Service desk, systems/cloud management, monitoring, patch coordination, asset lifecycle, vendor coordination, reporting, agreed safeguards.
- **Resilience & Security Plus.** Foundation plus security workstream, vulnerability/risk tracking, recovery validation, incident coordination, awareness support, executive reviews. Validated controls only.

**Add-ons:** risk analysis support, policy/process, roadmap and budget, disaster recovery, migrations, identity/endpoint projects, service desk transition, project staffing.

**Commercial guardrails**
- Scoped discovery worksheet and bill of materials
- Separate recurring service from projects, licenses, third-party fees
- State per-site / user / device assumptions
- Show onboarding costs, SLAs, exclusions, prerequisites
- Security and legal review of every BAA and incident term
- Never reuse small-business pricing for FQHC proposals

## 4. Route to Market

### Channel First
1. Map PCAs and HCCNs in our geography (NACHC directory).
2. Offer education, not pitches. Example: *"From risk analysis to a fundable 90-day resilience roadmap."*
3. Follow with a checklist or workshop, then a discovery invitation. Imply no endorsement by HRSA, NACHC, PCAs, or HCCNs.
4. Referral or subcontract deals only after due diligence and written agreement.

### Direct Account-Based Outreach
Build the list from HRSA data, websites, filings, job postings, board materials, partner referrals. Log source, signal, date, sites, complexity, contacts, next step in CRM. Never buy or scrape patient data.

**15-day cadence**

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Personalized email to CEO/COO or IT leader, tied to a public signal |
| 3 | Call and voicemail; ask who owns IT resilience |
| 6 | Send a useful one-page checklist or webinar invite |
| 9 | Second role (CFO/COO or IT) with a different angle |
| 13 | Call with one specific hypothesis |
| 15 | Close the loop; move to nurture |

**First-touch email**

> **Subject:** A practical resilience roadmap for [Health Center]
>
> Hi [Name], I work with ALLSTAR Partners, a managed IT provider that bundles support, systems management, security, and recovery services. We're speaking with community health centers about prioritizing technology risk without creating a second full-time job for a lean IT team. I noticed [specific, publicly verifiable signal]. Would 25 minutes be useful to compare your priorities against a simple 90-day roadmap? If the timing isn't right, I'm happy to send the checklist we use. — [Rep]

### Content Themes
- What to ask your MSP about ePHI access, backup, incident handling, subcontractors
- Turning a risk analysis into an owned, budgeted plan
- Clinical continuity mapping: what recovers first
- A board-ready IT and cyber dashboard
- Sorting shared services, HCCN support, and your MSP without overlap

**Regulatory language:** HHS describes the Security Rule update as a *proposal*. The current rule remains in effect, and the rulemaking is listed as a long-term action. Re-verify before every campaign.

## 5. Sales Process

| Stage | Exit criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Targeted | ICP fit, one verified trigger, named contacts, next action |
| 2. Discovery | Sponsor, pain, current state, timing, decision process, budget path, technical evaluator |
| 3. Qualified | Measurable issue, decision-maker access, delivery capacity, procurement path |
| 4. Review | Written scope, data rules, participants, acceptance criteria |
| 5. Solution design | Findings mapped to catalog, responsibility split, pricing, SLAs |
| 6. Proposal | Business case, implementation plan, terms, decision date |
| 7. Commit | Signed terms and BAA, onboarding plan, baseline, escalation matrix |
| 8. Expand / renew | Quarterly reviews, roadmap, budget |

### Discovery Questions
- **Mission:** Which services and sites depend most on technology? What downtime hurts patient care most?
- **IT model:** Who owns IT? What sits with HCCN, EHR, cloud, telecom vendors? Where do tickets stall?
- **Risk:** Last risk analysis? Who owns remediation? Are backups tested? Recovery priorities? Last incident exercise?
- **Access:** Onboarding/offboarding, privileged and remote access, training, third-party access?
- **Decision:** What makes this urgent? Budget cycle? Approvals? Who can veto?

### Qualification (MEDDICC-lite)
Metrics · Economic buyer · Decision criteria · Decision process · Identified pain · Champion · Competition (incumbent, hiring, HCCN, or doing nothing).

**Rule:** A quote request alone does not advance a deal. No sponsor, no defined problem, no decision access, or no delivery fit means nurture or decline.

---

## 6. Business Case

Build around total operating value, not unsupported breach-cost or insurance-savings claims. Use the customer's own baseline: support hours, ticket backlog, interruptions, vendor coordination, recovery gaps, aging devices, current provider cost.

**ROI narrative:** What is unowned today? What capacity returns to IT and clinical operations? What shrinks repeat issues and outage time? What becomes predictable? What stays the center's responsibility?

**Path:** Stabilize → close top gaps → mature governance. Present a lean must-do, a recommended package, and optional items. Tie to grant funding only after the center's finance/compliance team confirms eligibility.

## 7. 90-Day Pilot Targets

*Learning targets, not forecasts. Recalibrate after two months of data.*

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Named ICP accounts researched and tiered | 50 (100% with source, trigger, next step) |
| New stakeholder conversations | 8–12 / month |
| Qualified discoveries | 4–6 / month |
| Scoped reviews or workshops | 2–4 / month |
| Proposals | 2–3 / month |
| Wins by day 90 | 1–2, if cycle and capacity allow |

**Track:** conversion by tier, source, geography, role; time to discovery, proposal, decision; win/loss reasons; recurring revenue and margin; sales-engineering hours; onboarding effort; renewal; SLA outcomes.

**Rhythm:** 30-minute weekly pipeline review · monthly sales/delivery feedback · quarterly segment and package review. Sales never outruns delivery. A deal counts only after scope, margin, resourcing, and security obligations clear review.

## 8. First 90 Days

**Days 1–15: Readiness**
- Name executive owner, sales owner, delivery lead, security/compliance reviewer
- Validate coverage, response times, escalation, monitoring, backups, subcontractors, insurance, BAA process, references
- Define review scope, data rules, pricing logic, exclusions
- Build responsibility matrix, discovery guide, qualification checklist, proposal template, sample roadmap
- Build first 50-account list; map 10 channel partners

**Days 16–30: First conversations**
- Train team on FQHC context and careful HIPAA language
- Start Tier 1 outreach; hold two partner meetings
- Publish one checklist; schedule one webinar or roundtable
- Run 8–10 discovery conversations; log objections

**Days 31–60: Deliver and convert**
- Run first reviews; deliver readouts promptly
- Convert findings to phased options with budget assumptions
- Capture effort data to test margin
- Secure a partner-hosted session or referral motion
- Narrow messaging to the trigger that opens doors

**Days 61–90: Prove repeatability**
- Close pilots where fit exists; use documented onboarding
- Request references only after measurable, approved outcomes
- Compare expected and actual effort, margin, scope exceptions
- Decide: scale geography, hire FQHC role, formalize channel agreements
- Set next-quarter targets from observed conversion and capacity

## 9. Risks and Controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Compliance overclaim | Legal/security review; separate law, guidance, voluntary goals; recheck rule status each campaign |
| Vendor assurance gap | Readiness checklist before outbound; no unsupported promises |
| Review turns into free audit | Written scope, exclusions, authorized testing only, data minimization |
| Operational overload | Capacity gate at proposal; defined service hours |
| Long procurement | Map decision sequence early; phased options; disciplined nurture |
| Channel conflict | Co-map roles; fill gaps, don't displace |
| Weak proof | Substantiated references and artifacts; no invented credentials |

---

## 10. Decisions for Leadership

1. Confirm geography, delivery capacity, account target, and whether FQHC is a dedicated vertical.
2. Approve review scope, price/waiver rules, deliverables, liability boundaries.
3. Approve BAA, security questionnaire, incident-notification, subcontractor, insurance, and data-handling positions.
4. Choose packaging: standard core plus FQHC modules, or custom-only until repeatable.
5. Set pilot budget, owners, CRM stages, 90-day review date.
6. Decide which claims ALLSTAR can substantiate publicly; close gaps before broad launch.
